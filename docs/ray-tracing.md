# Ray tracing

The ray traced passes of the HDR main view (R14-R20 of `neo/renderer/RenderBackend.h`), in the order a frame needs them: read it before changing the G-buffer, the GPU scene, the acceleration structures, the traced shadows, their history, the indirect light, the reflections or the debug views.

## The switch and its cost

Ray tracing (`r_rayTracing 1`, archived; Settings > System > Ray Tracing, dimmed where `idRenderSystem::HasRayTracing` is false: no HDR scene or a GPU without `caps.rayTracing`): the HDR main view traces its shadows, one bounce of indirect light with the emissive surfaces and glossy reflections (`r_rtShadows`, `r_rtIndirect`, `r_rtReflections`, all 1; below). `0` traces nothing, `r_rtDebug` and `r_rtStats` included: no GPU scene, no G-buffer but for `r_gbufferDebug`, stencil shadows, the frames of before. `R_DefaultRayTracingSettings` reads it each frame, so the switch needs no restart: the GPU scene goes on from what it held, the history starts again (`_xc_rt_switch` flips it on 01_camera). Calibrated at `r_rtIndirectScale`, `r_rtEmissiveScale` and `r_rtFillLightScale` 1: the demos' mean brightness is 1.05 (median of the frames; 0.995-1.13 per demo) of the frames without it, and Doom 3 stays dark; `r_rtFillLightScale 0.5` left hell and delta darker than before. Cost on an RX 7900 XTX (`r_pix`, median GPU ms of the back end's frame, against `r_rayTracing 0`): at 1280x720 1.5-2.5 against 0.3-1.2 (the ray traced passes 0.95-1.5, the scene 0.1-0.25, the traced interactions 0.2-0.6 more than the stencil ones); at 3840x2160 12.4-18.4 against 1.2-2.7 (the passes 9.6-13.6, the interactions 4.9-7.0), where it is the frame (`research/perf.md`).

## Thin G-buffer (R14)

Thin G-buffer (R14 of `RenderBackend.h`, `renderer/RayTracingSettings.h`), the input of the ray traced passes: in the HDR main view, after the depth pass, `RB_FillGBuffer` (`tr_backend_draw.cpp`, `MRB_GBUFFER`) draws the opaque surfaces again with `gbuffer`/`gbuffer_skinned` (rows of the program table of our own, made after id's), depth EQUAL without a depth write (a perforated surface once per polygon offset of the alpha-tested stages the depth pass drew: a burning monster's two), into four targets of the scene's size and samples (`d3d12TargetId_t::GBuffer`): the octahedral world normal (RG16 SNORM), F0 and roughness (RGBA8 sRGB: the first specular stage times its colour, 0 for the weapon, GUIs and subviews; the gloss is sqrt( F0 luminance ) × `r_gbufferGloss`), the motion in pixels (RG16F, GL's +y up) against each draw's previous MVP, and the albedo (RGBA8 sRGB: the first diffuse stage times its colour, as an interaction takes it but without the light and the vertex colour, black without a diffuse stage; its alpha says what was drawn: 0 nothing, 1 a surface, 0.5 one under the weapon's depth hack, whose clip z is a quarter of the true one). An MD5 uploads its previous joints right after the current ones in the same joint block (`R_JointMotionLayout`, `rpJointMotion`; 136 joints fill gbuffer_skinned's `JOINT_VECTORS 816`); `idRenderEntityLocal::motionMatrix` keeps the model matrix of the frame before, so a still entity under a still camera moves exactly 0. Nothing of it runs while no consumer asks (`viewDef_t::rayTracing.gbuffer`): the frames are then those without it. `r_gbufferDebug` 1-5 shows the normal, F0, roughness, motion or albedo after the resolve (`_x9_gbuffer`); its raw view shows the AMD depth-tile flake as black 4x2 blocks along edges, in a few frames per demo. `_xb_gbuffer_burn` burns an imp and a wraith in the normals view.

## GPU scene (R15)

GPU scene (R15 of `RenderBackend.h`, `renderer/GpuScene.h`), what the ray traced passes trace: one `idGpuScene` per render world gathers, after `R_AddModels` of a main view that asks (`viewDef_t::rayTracing.scene`), the entities of its connected areas (static vertex-cache models and the MD5s skinned on the GPU this frame; no translucent or deformed surface, weapon or other view's surface) into a slot per entityDef: a range of 288-byte geometry records (offsets into the static buffers, flags, the bump, diffuse, specular, emissive and alpha textures with their stage matrices and colours), plus the frame's instances (transform, mask, BLAS key, joints; a slot's surfaces are up to eight instances, by side, by the material's `noSelfShadow` and by whether they are drawn, `GpuScene_OrderByGroup`; what only casts, a material without a stage and with `forceShadows`, is `GEO_SHADOW_ONLY` and its instance lacks `MASK_VISIBLE`, so no ray but a shadow ray meets it, and so does every instance of an entity the view suppresses without its shadow, the player's body under `g_showPlayerShadow 1`) and lights, written whole each update into a buffer of their own. A record's parms are the entity's as a draw evaluates them (`R_EntityShaderParms`: a light's model follows its light's `referenceShader`). A record goes out only when it differs from what the GPU holds. A full update follows the first frame, a level load, a new set of static buffers (`vertexCache.StaticGeneration()`) and a refused update (asked for once, `gpuSceneRecovery_t`: with `com_smp 1` the delta gathered meanwhile is refused too). Every view of a frame that sees other areas gathers again (a portal sky's view comes before the player's) and its update holds every write of the frame so far, after `base`, the last update of the frame before. Only the HDR main view sends (`RB_UpdateGpuScene`, which loads an image no draw has bound yet): the backend applies a full update or one whose writes follow what it holds (`base` <= its sequence < the update's), takes the same one again as a no-op (the second eye), and refuses a gap, another scene or a delta after a SAMPLED texture that a held record views died (`idD3D12TextureStore::SampledDestroyed`; any other texture, a video's, changes nothing); nothing may be traced until the next full one. `idD3D12GpuScene` keeps the records in a DEFAULT buffer with a raw SRV that only grows (a GPU copy into the bigger one) and a CPU copy for the acceleration structures. `r_rtStats 1` builds it and prints `rt scene <id>.<sequence>: instances, geometries, written, capacity, lights, refused` once a second, then what the acceleration structures made of it, and for every refused update why (`gpuSceneRefusal_t`).

## Acceleration structures

Acceleration structures (`D3D12/d3d12_RayTracing.h`), built as render graph passes after each applied update (the no-op of the second eye builds nothing): `rt skin` (`internal/rtSkin.hlsl` skins every skinned instance into a buffer of the frame in idDrawVert's layout, normal and tangent normalized), `rt blas` (new static BLAS with PREFER_FAST_TRACE, every skinned one with PREFER_FAST_BUILD), `rt blas compact`, `rt tlas` (InstanceID the instance's first geometry record, its mask) and `rt blas sizes`. A static BLAS is one per BLAS key, checked against its geometries (another model under a held key waits), kept 600 builds after its last instance and dropped with the static generation; after a full update a build makes every one the scene lacks, so that frame traces all its casters, in batches of 32 MB of scratch that take the same bytes one after another (a UAV barrier between two); after a delta new ones up to 32 MB, the instances of the rest wait. Compacted sizes are read once `WaitedFence()` passes their list, never by polling, so two runs record the same commands; WARP's compacted sizes are its built ones. The instance table (`d3d12RtInstance_t`: first geometry, count, skinned offset, scene instance) is a raw SRV by InstanceIndex. The graph puts a UAV barrier after every imported structure it wrote last, so a later graph of the list reads it built. A WRL `ComPtr`'s `&` releases what it holds: take its address with `std::addressof`.

## Light target (R16)

Light target (R16 of `RenderBackend.h`), for the traced lights: between `BeginTracedLights( rect )` and `EndTracedLights()`, in the HDR scene's linear phase, a draw's first colour output goes to the scene and its second to a second RGBA16F surface of the scene's size and samples (`idD3D12SceneTarget::Lights`, made at the first bracket, cleared to zero inside `rect`), both through the draw's one blend. Only programs with two outputs draw there and nothing clears. An internal pipeline replaces, adds or multiplies (`d3d12PassBlend_t`).

## Render programs that trace (R17)

Render programs that trace (R17 of `RenderBackend.h`; `traces` in the program table: `interaction_rt` and `interaction_rt_skinned`, rows of our own, which the traced lights draw with): the pixel stage includes `include/rtDraw.hlsli`, whose cbuffer at b4 is 9 root constants (`d3d12TraceResources_t`: the heap indices of the TLAS, the records, the instance table, the static vertices and indexes, the skinned vertices, the noise and a linear sampler, then flags), and traces with the inline `RayQuery` of `include/raytracing.hlsli`. The compiler refuses b4 in any other stage (`hlslParms_t::TRACING`, from `D3D12_ProgramParms`) and checks its layout as it does b3's. The draw recorder asks its trace source for every draw of such a program (`idD3D12RtPasses::DrawResources`: the transitions of the scene's buffers, again in every list since they decay, and the walk of `MakeTexturesReadable` only when the scene or `idD3D12TextureStore::StateChanges()` moved) and sets b4 when it differs; all zero (`rtFlags` bit 0 clear: nothing built, an update refused) means no ray. A staged buffer has one read state, vertex or index buffer plus ALL_SHADER_RESOURCE, so a draw and a ray read the static buffers in one list. `interaction_rt` is `interaction` (both are `include/interaction.hlsli`: the same colour into the scene) plus a shadow ray from the pixel's world position (the rows of `rpModelMatrix`, at the centroid under MSAA, off the surface by 0.02 plus a tenth of the pixel's footprint on the side the eye sees; toward a point behind the surface it comes back through the pixel's own point) toward a point of the light's disk (`rpRtLight`: world origin and radius; `rpRtShadow`: the casters' mask and the noise index; a surface that faces the light takes a point behind its own plane from across the disk), ending a radius short of it; where a caster stops it the colour also goes to the light target. The noise (`include/rtNoise.hlsli`) is a 64x64 blue-noise texture of two channels (`renderer/BlueNoise.h`, written by `tools/rt/make_bluenoise.py`, the same bytes on every run: change the script, never its output), shifted by the R2 sequence of the index in integers. A ray that needs several pairs (its direction, a light, a point of the light's disk) takes each with a step of its own (`RtNoise( ..., pair )`: the plastic number's, then square roots of primes): with one step the pairs move together, and what depends on two of them at once keeps an error at any number of frames (grain that `r_rtReference` never lost). `Warp_RtDraw`/`Gpu_RtDraw` draw a floor under a local and a global caster through the recorder: hard and soft shadows, the masks, nothing to trace, a submit between two draws.

## Traced shadows

Traced shadows (`r_rtShadows`, 1 by default; needs `r_useGPUSkinning 1`): `RB_UpdateGpuScene` says whether the frame may trace (the update applied, `gpuSceneStats_t::traceable`, and no BLAS that the last full update could not build still waits, `gpuSceneStats_t::lacking`; after 60 gathers `gpuSceneSettle_t` gives up with a warning, and those instances cast nothing until they are built, as one that joins a delta does), and `backEnd.tracedShadows` then holds for the HDR main view; any other frame or view keeps its stencil shadows. Once a frame has traced a world's main view, the front end makes no shadow volumes for the lights that view traces (`rtShadowSettings_t::volumes`, set in `RenderScene` from `tr.tracedScene`: the scene and update the back end's last frame traced, `rtShadowLatch_t`, copied in `SwapCommandBuffers_FinishCommandBuffers` so that it is the same value whatever the threads' timing; after a full update not before the back end traced that one): no shadow surfaces and no prelight volumes, while a shadow-only MD5 still gets its joints uploaded (the GPU scene takes it by them). Subviews and a view drawn for the main one (`RenderScene( view, true )`: the portal sky's) keep their volumes (`KeepStencil`), and so does a second view of a world in a frame or one after a GUI view of the frame that draws (`rtMainViewForecast_t`: the wipe at a level's end draws the game twice in one frame). The front end marks all of these (`viewDef_t::notMainView`) and `RB_FindHdrMainView` passes them over, so the first view stays the main one; a marked view after it is drawn into the output as an LDR view and gathers nothing. A frame that then cannot trace (a refused update) draws its lights without a ray and keeps the shadows its history holds (`backEnd.tracedHeld`, `rtView_t::held`; without a history it is lit whole); the views two frames on have their volumes again. `r_rtShadowVolumes 1` makes the volumes all the same (the frames are the same bytes, a frame that cannot trace draws them: what they cost the front end), and `r_rtStats 1` prints `rt shadows: frames, traced, without volumes, held` once a second, plus a line for every frame that is not traced without volumes (the first two of a level, a held one). A view that traces also leaves out a shadow-casting light whose area is not connected (`AddAreaViewLights`), as it always did the lights without shadows: the GPU scene holds none of that area's walls, so light through the window of a closed door is lost. `RB_DrawInteractions` first draws, in one bracket of the light target (`RB_DrawTracedLights`), the opaque interactions of every light that casts shadows (no ambient, fog or blend light) with `interaction_rt`, depth EQUAL and no stencil, under the light's scissor and depth bounds: the local ones (`noSelfShadow`) with the mask `MASK_SHADOW_GLOBAL`, the others with `MASK_SHADOW`, the noise index 97 times the light's index in its world (`viewLight_t::lightIndex`: no other light of the view's list changes it), plus the view's sequence when the shadows keep a history. `ResolveTracedShadows` (`idD3D12RtPasses::Shadows`, raster passes of the graph, the filter of the next point between them) then stores per pixel and channel what the shadows leave of the lights, ( scene - light target ) / scene (`internal/rtShadowRatio.hlsl`: the difference first, so all taken is exactly 0 and nothing taken exactly 1; a channel no light reaches takes the others' luminance-weighted part; alpha the unshadowed luminance; over the pixel's samples under MSAA), and multiplies the scene by it (`rtShadowApply.hlsl`). The lights without shadows and all translucent interactions follow as before. `r_rtDebug 6` shows the stored part in place of the lights, blue where no traced light reaches. A shadow ray follows the stencil volumes, which come from the triangles that face away from the light: `RtOccluded` culls back faces, and the ray starts on the side of the surface the eye sees, so a surface with the light behind it shadows itself, as a volume's cap does (a light inside a closed mesh, a fireball in a monster's hand, stays shadowed; the triangles of a mesh that face away from the light stay dark, faceted as id's). With `r_rtLightSize 0` the frames are the stencil ones but for the edges; expected differences: alpha-tested casters cast their holes, a projected light's rays pass the space before `light_start`, `suppressShadowInLightID` is no ray mask, what is outside the GPU scene casts nothing, and a hull that draws nothing (`textures/common/shadow`) casts also where the stencil shadows never drew it: as a part of an entity (the cacodemon's shadows its mouth and the hands under it), and from a light that moves. A light with a size gives a grainy penumbra without the filter, and a lamp closer than its radius to a wall loses the part of its disk behind that wall for the surfaces beside it. A dim traced light inside a much brighter one's shadow is quantized by the bright sum's half-float step, about 1e-3 of it. `Warp_RtShadows`/`Gpu_RtShadows` resolve one and two lights, a local surface, a noshadows quad, one that faces the light, the floor's own shadow of a light behind it, the shown picture and a light of radius 6 over 64 noise indices against the hidden part of its disk (`idTestTracedFloor`, which `RtDraw` shares).

## History and the shadows' filter (R18)

History (R18 of `RenderBackend.h`, `D3D12/d3d12_RtHistory.h`): shared code numbers the HDR main view's frames per eye (`RB_BeginMainViewHistory`, `backEnd.viewEpoch` and `viewSequence`: a view follows the one before when the viewID, the size and the frame number plus 1 agree and the time did not go back; anything else starts an epoch at sequence 0) and `rtView_t` carries both with this frame's and the previous world-to-clip matrix. A pass reads only what sequence - 1 of its epoch stored. A screenshot reads the history and stores nothing (`rtView_t::store`), so the frame after it is the one without it. A frame that traced nothing (`rtView_t::held`, the traced shadows' frame without volumes) shows what its history holds without counting a frame; without one the shadows' resolve lights it whole and the other passes add nothing. The filter of the traced signals is NVIDIA NRD (below), which keeps their histories; `r_rtDenoiser 0` shows every frame's rays as they are. `r_rtReference 1`, for a still camera, shows in its place the plain mean of a signal's rays over the frames, unfiltered: `idD3D12RtHistory` holds per eye and signal (the shadows' ratio, the indirect light's amount and chroma, the reflections) a pair of RGBA32F surfaces (a half float stops counting at 2048) with the epoch, the sequence and the frames each holds (the same sequence again reads and writes the same two: the second eye's no-op, a view drawn twice), made only for the reference and dropped at a resize, a level load and the shutdown. `rt <signal> mean` (`internal/rtAccumulate.hlsl`) adds the frame's rays to the mean before, keeps it through a held frame and starts it again when the eye moves (`worldToClip` against `prevWorldToClip`: what the rays see depends on where it is). Until NRD the passes had a filter of their own, temporal and a-trous passes over a history with guides of the G-buffer (the NRD section compares the two; `git log -- base/shaders/internal/rtTemporal.hlsl` finds its last state). The demos draw 4 frames per tic (`vdemo_fps 240`), so their histories hold a quarter of the game time a 60 fps player's do: judge the look with `+set vdemo_fps 60`. `Warp_RtHistory`/`Gpu_RtHistory` draw the traced floor with perspective, depth and a G-buffer over frames: the reference's error against the hidden part of the light's disk after 1, 4, 16 and 32 frames, a sequence drawn twice, a screenshot, a moved eye, a held frame with and without a mean, and SIGMA (below).

## Indirect light (R19)

Indirect light (R19 of `RenderBackend.h`, `internal/rtIndirect.hlsl`, `include/rtBounce.hlsli`), `r_rtIndirect` (1 by default; it needs no `r_rtShadows`): after the reflections and before the gamma phase (`DrawRayTracedIndirect`), one bounce for the HDR main view on a grid of a texel per stride pixels a side (`r_rtIndirectResolution` 0.5: every second pixel; a texel is its block's first pixel). `rt guides` of that grid hold the plane the depth gives (`RtDepthNormal` of `include/rtView.hlsli`), not the bumped normal, so the bumps of a wall share one signal. `rt indirect` sends a ray by the cosine about that plane, up to `r_rtIndirectDistance`; the hit is lit by `RtShadeSampled` (`include/rtLighting.hlsli`): of the lights that reach it one, picked by what it adds unshadowed (one number, a reservoir of one) and scaled to the sum, with one ray toward a point of its disk (`RtDiskOccluded`: also for a light that casts no stencil shadow, the flashlight, whose bounce must not come through walls; not for an ambient light), without specular, plus the emissive stage times `r_rtEmissiveScale`. So the emissive surfaces light through these rays; no list of them exists. A ray brings at most `r_rtIndirectClamp` (8) times the exposed scene's white (in the lab's emissive room 0, 2, 8 and 32 give the same frames but the level's first one: a net for a higher emissive scale and brighter lights). A texel keeps, unexposed (2 x RGBA16F), the luminance (Y of YCoCg), that times the ray's direction, the chroma and how far the ray went. NRD filters them (below), and `rt indirect upsample` (raster) gives every pixel the texels of its surface among the four around it (by the guides: `include/rtGuide.hlsli` packs the plane's normal and clip w, and beside it the change of 1 / w per texel, which is linear over a plane on the screen; among the twelve around those for something thinner than the grid), by its own bumped normal: `RtBounceShare` reads the kept amount and mean direction as an even glow of the half sphere plus light from one direction, exact for either and any mix, and a flat surface takes exactly 1; times the albedo, `r_rtIndirectScale` and preExposure, added to the scene. `r_rtDebug 7` puts it in the scene's place as a white surface takes it. `r_rtFillLightScale` (1, 0..1) dims id's stand-ins for bounced light while the indirect light is on (`R_FillLight`: ambient lights and point lights without shadows; a projected one without shadows is a lamp of its own), in the main view's interactions and for the rays. A frame that cannot trace takes what NRD holds; `r_rtHistory 0` and `r_rtReference 1` work as for the shadows. GPU times and memory: the NRD section. Known limits: a surface one pixel wide gets its light only where the grid meets it (a dashed line in `r_rtDebug 7`; `r_rtIndirectResolution 1` traces every pixel); NRD's radii, made for 1080p and more, carry light across the line of an indirect shadow (see the NRD section); with the indirect light on the eye adaptation meters what was black before, so a dark room's exposure rises within its 0.5 EV. `Warp_RtIndirect`/`Gpu_RtIndirect` (1 and 4 samples): under an emissive sky the floor takes the sky's light at strides 1, 2 and 4, added, alone, scaled and clamped, a leaning normal its share; a room beside a lit wall against the form factor on the CPU (the means within 3 %), nothing behind a black plate and behind the wall, two lamps their sum; the same bytes without a history whatever came before; the reference's means for another epoch, after a screenshot, in a frame that cannot trace and without a mean.

## Reflections (R20)

Reflections (R20 of `RenderBackend.h`; `D3D12/d3d12_RtPasses.h`, `internal/rtReflect.hlsl`, `rtComposite.hlsl`, `include/rtReflection.hlsli`, `include/rtLighting.hlsli`): `r_rtReflections` (1 by default) traces, after `RB_DrawInteractions` and before the gamma phase (`DrawRayTracedReflections`), a ray per pixel of the HDR main view whose G-buffer reflects (F0 above 0, roughness below `r_rtReflectionRoughness` 0.6, faded in over 0.15 below it): from the point the depth gives, off the plane its neighbours hold (`RtDepthNormal`), up to `r_rtReflectionDistance`. `rt reflect` stores what the ray met, before the surface's Fresnel, and how far it went. A pixel smoother than `r_rtReflectionMirror` (0.08), and every pixel with `r_rtReflectionGlossy 0`, is a mirror: its ray leaves about the G-buffer's normal (kept above that plane), the same every frame, with no history and no filter. Its hit is lit as the linear phase lights a pixel (interaction.hlsl's math in the hit's object space, the modifiers decoded as products, ambient lights without specular, vertex colours white, the emissive stage added in linear) by the first `r_rtReflectionLights` lights that reach it. Those are the GPU scene's `gpuSceneLight_t`: every light of the connected areas but fog and blend lights and those whose centre lies in an area that is not connected (behind a closed door: none of that area's walls are in the scene to stop it), one per enabled stage, projection and falloff in world space with the stage matrix baked in. A light has a size: the spawn arg `light_size` is the radius in units of the disk that emits, by default 2 % of the light's reach (the largest `light_radius`, the length of a projected light's `light_target`) within 2..10 units, all times `r_rtLightSize` (0 = points); `R_LightEmitterRadius` gives it to `viewLight_t` and `gpuSceneLight_t`, and a savegame or a render demo does not hold it (idLight reads its spawn arg again). The traced shadows, a bounce and a glossy ray sample the disk; a mirror's shadow rays aim at its centre. Each casts one (`r_rtReflectionShadows`) from the side of the hit that the reflection came to: `RtOccluded` culls back faces, since Doom 3's shadow volumes come from the triangles that face away from the light (a hit on one is in its own shadow), and skips geometry without `GEO_CASTS_SHADOW` (a lamp's noshadows model), which the BLAS therefore holds not opaque. A hit on a `noSelfShadow` material traces only the casters that are not (`RtShadowMask`, mask bit 2 `MASK_SHADOW_GLOBAL`), as the stencil shadows draw the local interactions before the local shadows. A rougher pixel's ray leaves about a facet of its GGX lobe (alpha the roughness squared, the facets drawn as the eye sees them, `RtGgxVisibleNormal`; without the last tenth of the lobe, `RT_REFLECT_LOBE`, whose rays land lobes away as lone dots) and its hit is lit by `RtShadeSampled` as a bounce's is, here with the highlight: one light for all that reach it and a ray toward its disk. NRD's REBLUR_SPECULAR filters the glossy pixels' rays (below). `rt composite` (raster) multiplies by Schlick's Fresnel capped by the smoothness, the fade, `r_rtReflectionScale` and preExposure and adds that to SceneColor's RGB; `r_rtDebug 5` puts it in place of the lights' sum. A frame that cannot trace shows what NRD holds of the glossy pixels and nothing in a mirror (with `r_rtReference 1` every pixel's mean). `r_rtHistory 0` is NRD's spatial filter alone on the same noise every frame; `r_rtReference 1` the glossy pixels' plain mean. Measured at 1280x720 on an RX 7900 XTX: the rays alone with `r_rtReflectionGlossy 0` 0.08-0.25 ms of GPU time on the retail demos and 0.1 ms in the gallery and the gloss room; what NRD adds is in the NRD section. With `r_rtReflectionGlossy 0` the frames are the mirrors of before up to 1 of 255 (the radiance is stored as a half before the Fresnel) and a lone pixel on a reflected edge now and then. Known limits: what a reflection shows is lit by the lights and its own emission, by no bounce, so with the indirect light on a mirror's image is darker than the room; a hole of the G-buffer reflects nothing for its frame; a retail material's roughness comes from its specular map's luminance (`r_gbufferGloss`) and varies per texel. `Warp_RtReflection`/`Gpu_RtReflection` run the mirror end to end: a mirror floor under a lit ceiling, a two-sided shadow caster, a noshadows one, an emissive panel, a rough and a black band. `Warp_RtGlossy`/`Gpu_RtGlossy` (the sky also at 4 samples): under an emissive sky every roughness reflects the sky times its Fresnel, in one frame, through NRD and in the reference; under a ceiling of an emissive and a black half a rough band shows the part of its lobe that meets the emissive half, which the CPU sums over the facets (a column's mean within 0.03 in the reference); the same bytes without a history; the reference of an eye that moved is that frame alone; a frame that cannot trace shows the reference's mean; and under a red and a blue lamp at one place a pixel is as red as it is blue (the noise pairs' own steps).

## NRD (r_rtDenoiser)

NVIDIA NRD 4.18.0 (`D3D12/d3d12_Nrd.h`, `USE_NRD`, `renderCaps_t::nrd`) is the filter of the traced signals: `r_rtDenoiser` 1 (the default) filters with NRD, 0 not at all. Without NRD in the build (`-DD3BFG_NRD=OFF`) 1 warns once and filters nothing. The glossy reflections go through REBLUR_SPECULAR, the indirect light through REBLUR_DIFFUSE_SH, the shadows through SIGMA_SHADOW_TRANSLUCENCY. `r_rtReference 1` shows its plain mean whatever `r_rtDenoiser` says. Measured at 1280x720 on an RX 7900 XTX over 9 demos, NRD's three filters cost 0.64 ms of GPU time on average (0.47 in the gloss room, 0.76 in `04_lighting`), 1.3 ms at 1920x1080, as much as the own filter they replaced; their textures take 136 MB at 1280x720 and 306 MB at 1920x1080, 2.3 times the own filter's. Against `r_rtReference` on still stops NRD lies 2 to 3 times farther than the own filter did (glossy floor 1.7-2.7 of 255 against 0.6-1.0, indirect light 4.5-5.1 against 2.1-2.2, shadows 0.4-1.8 against 0.4-0.5): its radii are made for 1080p and more and for many rays, not one ray a pixel of one light picked at random. In motion it is the better one: no dark patches where a history starts again after a cut (the own filter's lasted 5 to 60 tics), a light that goes out is gone 3 times sooner, the lab's rotor shows no blocks; it trails a moving beacon's beam (`04_lighting`) and smears a reflection under a fast eye. NRD hands out compute dispatches of its own DXIL, each a COMPUTE pass of the render graph (`docs/d3d12.md` for the root signatures and tables); its instances, pools and inputs live per signal and eye at the signal's size, the viewport's or the indirect light's grid (a screenshot gets instances of its own for its frame), dropped with the history. `nrd guides` (`internal/nrdGuides.hlsl`, sample 0 under MSAA) writes per eye, once a frame (again when the reflections' limits differ from the last; the shadows take the last), NRD's view z (the clip w; the weapon's a quarter of its depth's, the depth hack), the bumped normal with the roughness and a material of 1 where the pixel's reflection is glossy, 0 for a mirror or none, and the 2.5D motion (the G-buffer's, reversed into NRD's uv, z the clip w of the frame before minus this one, for a still world). The indirect light's grid has a set of its own: a texel per block's first pixel, the plane its depth holds, roughness 1, material 0. NRD's textures hold rows from the top, as the D3D clip space of the matrices it is given (GL's projection with its z made 0..1, GL's model view, which NRD takes as right-handed); `rtView_t` carries both and the view's time since the frame before (`frameMs`), so NRD runs no timer of its own and two runs give the same bytes. Lengths are world units, 39.37 to a metre: REBLUR's hit distance A is scaled by it, the denoising range is 1e6 units and the sky lies beyond it. `nrd reflect pack` hands a glossy pixel's ray to NRD as `REBLUR_FrontEnd_PackRadianceAndNormHitDist` (the radiance before the Fresnel, unexposed); `nrd reflect unpack` gives `rt composite` the filtered radiance of a glossy pixel and a mirror's ray as it is. The history follows the rules of R18: a new epoch, a skipped frame or `r_rtHistory 0` restarts NRD's accumulation, new textures clear it; a frame that cannot trace shows NRD's last output of the glossy pixels and nothing in a mirror.

The indirect light's grid goes through NRD. `rt indirect`'s chroma holds how far each ray went as well (its b; the longest ray on a miss); `nrd indirect pack` hands NRD the ray as it is, since R19's amount and chroma are REBLUR's SH layout already (the luminance, Co and Cg of YCoCg and the luminance times the direction), with the hit distance normalized at roughness 1. `nrd indirect unpack` turns NRD's output back into that layout, a frame in aux and nothing beyond the denoising range, and `rt indirect upsample` takes it as it takes the rays: R19's guides of the grid, the bumped normal of the pixel. NRD's texel centres are the grid's (a block's first pixel, a last column and row that may reach past the viewport): the grid instance's projections are mapped so that NRD's uv of a texel is where its pixel lies (x' = x / a + ( ( 1 - 2 b ) / a - 1 ) w with uv = a uv' + b, y alike from the top), and `motionVectorScale` takes the guides' motion in the viewport's uv to the grid's. A frame that cannot trace shows NRD's last output. Without a filter (`r_rtDenoiser 0`) every frame's rays go to the upsample as they are. NRD accumulates about half a second (REBLUR's default, `GetMaxAccumulatedFrameNum` of the smoothed frame rate, at most 63 frames). NRD needs the viewport whole in the scene: a viewport the scene cuts is not filtered. `r_rtNrdValidation` draws NRD's validation view of a signal (`internal/nrdValidation.hlsl`) over the resolved main view, 1 the glossy reflections', 2 the indirect light's (a texel a block); SIGMA has no such view. Its viewport 3 is black where the motion and the matrices agree for a still world (the gloss room's view shows it black, the floor's specular frames full; on the grid 01_camera's stays black in motion but for the weapon, which moves with the eye). `Warp_RtGlossy`/`Gpu_RtGlossy` run the NRD path: the sky within 0.6 % (REBLUR's YCoCg in half floats), also at 4 samples; the ceiling's lobe profile 6 pixels and more from the line (NRD's radii are pixels made for 1080p and more: over the test's 64 it blurs the line about 6 pixels wide; a column's mean 0.009, 0.13 and 0.16 off beyond them); the same bytes for another epoch and after a screenshot; a frame that cannot trace; under the eye that moves 4 units a frame NRD's reflection trails (0.78 of its half three pixels beside the line; the own filter, which looked the history up where the reflected image was, kept 0.99). `Warp_RtIndirect`/`Gpu_RtIndirect` do so for the indirect light, under an orthographic view NRD is given as such: the sky's even glow within 0.5 % at grids of 1, 2 and 4 pixels, the leaning bands within 4 %; in the room NRD's radii spread the light over the whole 64 pixels of floor, so what is checked is the floor's sum against the reference's (0.99 after a first frame, 0.94 after 32) and that the light stays where it falls (1.5 times brighter before the wall than beside it); the same bytes for another epoch, after a screenshot and in a frame that cannot trace.

The shadows go through SIGMA_SHADOW_TRANSLUCENCY as NRD's recipe for many lights has it. Under NRD (`rtShadowSettings_t::Penumbrae`, rpRtShadow.z) the shadow ray of `interaction_rt` finds the caster nearest to the surface rather than any, and the light target's alpha sums over the lights a caster shadows the luminance each takes times the width of its penumbra there (SIGMA_FrontEnd_PackPenumbra's for a local light: the disk's radius times the caster's distance over the disk's beyond it, at most 32768 units) times `PENUMBRA_SCALE` (1/64, so the sum fits a half float); the Lights target's second output adds its alpha where the blend keeps the destination's (`d3d12_StateMap.cpp`, independent blend), without NRD the draws leave it 0. `nrd shadow pack` hands NRD, rows from the top, the penumbra as that sum over the luminance taken, times `SIGMA_PENUMBRA` (a quarter: SIGMA blurs as wide as the penumbra it is given, which washes out the wide soft shadows of large lights; on `27_rt_shadows` a quarter keeps the thin poles' shadows before the 32-unit light and stays smooth, an eighth shows grain) (NRD_FP16_MAX where no caster stands; 0 where no traced light reaches, which SIGMA takes for a surface that faces away and leaves out of its blur) and the translucency as `rt shadow ratio`'s, what the shadows leave per channel; under MSAA the light target's samples are averaged. `nrd shadow unpack` gives `rt shadow apply` the yzw of SIGMA's output in the ratio's place. The guides are the reflections' set: SIGMA reads the normal and the view z. SIGMA keeps a few frames (`SIGMA_DEFAULT_ACCUMULATION_TIME` of the smoothed frame rate, at most 7). The history follows R18's rules as above; a frame that cannot trace shows SIGMA's last output. `Warp_RtShadows`/`Gpu_RtShadows` hold the light target's alpha to the wide light's geometry: the radius it gives lies between those of the disk's nearest and farthest points that face the pixel (2 % either way), and is 0 without NRD. `Warp_RtHistory`/`Gpu_RtHistory` run SIGMA over the reference's frames: a first frame 0.351 off the hidden part of the light (0.425 unfiltered: a quarter of the penumbra is a few of the test's 64 pixels), 16 frames 0.070 (within 1.4 times the reference's); a hard shadow changes only within 2.5 pixels of its edges (SIGMA's first 5x5 mixes a lit pixel's neighbours in: 114 pixels); the same bytes for another epoch, in a held frame and after a screenshot.

## Debug views

Ray traced debug views (`D3D12/d3d12_RtPasses.h`, `internal/rtDebug.hlsl`): `r_rtDebug` (`rtDebug_t`, any value gathers the GPU scene) traces a ray per pixel of the main view against what the last applied update built and draws it over the output after the resolve (`DrawRayTracingDebug`): 1 a colour per instance, 2 the rays against the depth (grey agrees, red misses a drawn surface, blue hits in front of one, yellow a back face), 3 the hit normal, 4 the diffuse colour, 5 the reflections alone, 6 what the traced shadows leave of the traced lights (blue where none reaches), and 7 the indirect light alone, as a white surface takes it (all three in the scene, before the resolve). `MRB_RT_SCENE` (update and builds) and `MRB_RAY_TRACING` time them in `r_pix`. `include/raytracing.hlsli` reads a hit through `ResourceDescriptorHeap` with inline `RayQuery` (`include/ycocg.hlsli` decodes the diffuse); textures live in ALL_SHADER_RESOURCE and `idD3D12GpuScene::MakeTexturesReadable` transitions the records' textures before a trace. The front face is the side Doom 3 draws (`TRIANGLE_FRONT_COUNTERCLOCKWISE`) and the rays cull back faces as the raster does: an instance holds two-sided geometries only and gets `CULL_DISABLE`, or none (an opaque back face never reaches the candidate loop, so only its instance can cull it). Perforated geometry is alpha-tested as the depth pass tests it: the GPU scene leaves out a surface whose stages are all conditioned off (a dead wraith's wings), a perforated one with no enabled alpha-tested stage is opaque, and enabled stages of one image and matrix take the lowest reference (a monster's two burn stages). AMD's driver gives a committed non-opaque hit the candidate's t plus TMin (`Gpu_RtHit`): `RtTrace` starts the query at TMin with a TMin of 0. Mode 2 may show red on the weapon, particles and translucent surfaces that write depth (`textures/glass/glass2`'s heat haze), all outside the GPU scene, and speckle on alpha-tested edges (the samplers differ). `_x8_rtdebug` runs the modes over the gallery, `_xa_rt_portalsky` modes 2 and 4 over `game/mp/d3xpdm4`, the retail map with a portal sky; `Warp_RtHit`/`Gpu_RtHit` hold the hit attributes, the culling, the alpha test and the sides a shadow ray stops at.
